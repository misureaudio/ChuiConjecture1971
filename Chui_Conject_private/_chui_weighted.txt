###### Abstract.

The goals of this paper are threefold. First, we show that a counterpart of the Newman bound related
to the Chui conjecture is valid in the case where the gradient of Coulomb potential is generated by
arbitrary positive charges placed at the boundary of the unit ball of ℝd\mathbb{R}^{d}. Second, we
prove that our bound is sharp in the two dimensional case. Finally, we discuss a related problem
where the unit charges are placed in the unit disc.

###### Key words and phrases: 

Coulomb potential, Chui’s conjecture, Cauchy transform, simplest fraction

30E20

## 1. Introduction

Let 𝔻\mathbb{D} denote the unit disc of the complex plane ℂ\mathbb{C}. Consider points
z1,z2,…,znz_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n} on the unit circle 𝕋=∂𝔻\mathbb{T}=\partial\mathbb{D}. The Chui
conjecture, formulated in [[Chu71]], suggests that the quantity

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┬─────
∫𝔻|∑k=1n1z−zk|𝑑m(z),\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{z-z_{k}}\Big|\,dm(z),│(1.1)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┴─────

where dm(z)dm(z) denotes the Lebesgue measure on the complex plane, attains its minimum if the
points z1,z2,…,znz_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n} are uniformly distributed on the unit circle, i.e., if
zk=e2πik/nz_{k}=e^{2\pi ik/n}. This conjecture has the following natural physical interpretation: in
order to minimize the average strength of the electrostatic field generated by nn unit charges on
the circle, the charges should be distributed uniformly.

Surprisingly, this natural conjecture remains open. Shortly after its formulation,
D.J. Newman [[New72]] proved that this average strength of an electrostatic field is bounded
from below, i.e., there exists an absolute constant c>0c>0 such that for every nn and any points
z1,z2,…znz_{1},z_{2},\ldots z_{n} on the unit circle 𝕋\mathbb{T} the following inequality holds:

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┬────
∫𝔻|∑k=1n1z−zk|𝑑m                                                                             │(1.2
(z)⩾c.\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{z-z_{k}}\Big|\,dm(z)\geqslant c.          │)   
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┴────

To be more precise, the above inequality holds with c=π/18c=\pi/18. This result is, of course,
weaker than Chui’s conjecture. Indeed, it is easy to see that if zk=e2πik/nz_{k}=e^{2\pi ik/n}, then
the corresponding quantity ([1.1]) in fact is bigger than π/18\pi/18.

Notice that even in the case of two charges Chui’s conjecture is a non-trivial question, as F.
Nazarov’s proof in [[MaOvFl]] suggests.

A more recent progress was obtained in the paper [[ABF21]] where the Chui conjecture was proved
if one replaces the L1L^{1} norm in the quantity ([1.1]) with the norm in certain Bergman
spaces.

Notice also that Chui’s conjecture is closely related to the topic in Approximation Theory called
the approximation by simplest fractions, see for instance papers [[BS23]], [[Bor16]],
[[Kom23]] and [[CZ23]].

In the present paper we study the following natural questions: what if we consider not unit but nn
different charges (of the same sign)? Also, what happens if we place them on the unit sphere of
ℝd\mathbb{R}^{d} (from the physical point of view, the case d=3d=3 is the most interesting)?
Clearly, the methods of Complex Analysis have certain limitations in the latter case.

It seems to be difficult to even formulate the analogue of Chui’s conjecture in this case since it
is not clear how the optimal distribution of the charges on the unit sphere should look like.
However, we are able to formulate and prove an analogue of the Newman bound in this case. We denote
by 𝕊d−1\mathbb{S}^{d-1} and 𝔹d\mathbb{B}^{d} the unit sphere and the unit ball in ℝd\mathbb{R}^{d},
respectively.

α1,…,αn>0\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}>0. Then

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┬───
∫𝔹d|∑k=1nαkxk−x|xk−x|d|𝑑m(x)⩾cd                                                               │(1.
∑k=1nαk1+2d∑k=1nαk2d,\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\frac{x_{k}-x}{|x_{k}-│3) 
x|^{d}}\right|\,dm(x)\geqslant                                                                │   
c_{d}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}^{1+\frac{2}{d}}}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}^{\frac{2}{d}}}, │   
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┴───

where cd>0c_{d}>0 depends only on dd, and dmdm stands for the Lebesgue measure on ℝd\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Passing to complex coordinates, it is easy to see that if d=2d=2 and
α1=α2=…=αn=1\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\ldots=\alpha_{n}=1, then this estimate coincides with Newman’s
bound ([1.2]).

Similar problems were considered by J. Korevaar and his coauthors, see for instance
papers [[Kor64]] and [[KM01]].

We also note that the estimate ([1.3]) in the special case d=3d=3 and unit values of
αk\alpha_{k} was posed as an open problem in [[Arr24]]. Once again, we mention that this case
has a clear physical interpretation: if x1,…,xn∈𝕊2x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}, then the
electrostatic field in the unit ball 𝔹3\mathbb{B}^{3} corresponding to the Coulomb potential

Therefore, our result states that the mean value of this electrostatic field is not too small, i.e.,
the charges cannot “compensate each other”.

## 2. Newman’s bound for arbitrary charges

In this section we prove Theorem [1.1]. The main idea of the proof is to consider appropriate
orthogonal projections on 2-dimensional subspaces of ℝd\mathbb{R}^{d} and then perform the
computations similar to what is done in [[New72]].

## 4. Concluding remarks and open problems

We finish the paper by proving Proposition 1.4 and posing several open questions.

### 4.1. Proof of Proposition [1.4]

###### Proof.

Denote Ua={z∈𝔻:|z−a|<δ} and Ub={z∈𝔻:|z−b|<δ}U_{a}=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z-a|<\delta\}\;\text{ and

### 4.2. Open questions

The following questions remain open.

First, we would like to ask whether the claim of our Theorem [1.1] in two dimensions holds in
the case where the poles zkz_{k} lie not only on the unit circle, but are also allowed to be inside
the unit disc. We know that this is true once the sum

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
∑k=1ndist⁡(zk,𝕋)

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

is small enough. Indeed, in this case one can push the poles zkz_{k} out to the boundary 𝕋\mathbb{T}

controlled, thanks to Proposition [1.4]. We also know this is true in case where the sum

Second, as mentioned above, we don’t know whether our bound in Theorem [1.1] is optimal in the
case where the dimension of the ambient space is three and higher, even if the weights are all equal
to 11.

Third, it is also interesting to find out if counterparts of Theorems [1.1] and [1.3] hold
for other energies such as ss-Riesz energies.

Finally, we would like to know what is the correct formulation of Chui’s weighted conjecture. We do
not know the answer even in the two dimensional case.
