###### Abstract.

We solve Chui’s conjecture on the simplest fractions (i.e., sums of Cauchy kernels with unit
coefficients) in weighted (Hilbert) Bergman spaces. Namely, for a wide class of weights, we prove
that for every NN, the simplest fractions with NN poles on the unit circle have minimal norm if and
only if the poles are equispaced on the circle. We find sharp asymptotics of these norms.
Furthermore, we describe the closure of the simplest fractions in weighted Bergman spaces, using an
L2L^{2} version of Thompson’s theorem on dominated approximation by simplest fractions.

## 1. Introduction

The starting point of our research is the following question: How to put NN point charges on the
unit circle 𝕋\mathbb{T} of the complex plane ℂ\mathbb{C} in order to minimize the average strength
of the corresponding electrostatic field in the unit disk 𝔻\mathbb{D}, assuming forces inversely
proportional to the distance? C. K. Chui [] conjectured in 1971 that this average strength is
minimal when the charges are equispaced on 𝕋\mathbb{T}, and, surprisingly, this very natural and
elementary conjecture is still open.

This and related questions call for the study of approximation properties of so-called simplest
fractions.

We mean by a simplest fraction (the term simple partial fraction is also used in the literature) a
rational function rr in the complex variable zz having the form

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
r⁡(z)=∑0≤k<N1z−ak,r(z)=\sum_{0\leq k<N}\frac{1}{z-a_{k}},
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

where NN is a positive integer, and aka_{k}, 0≤k<N0\leq k<N, are points in ℂ\mathbb{C}. Note that
the simplest fraction r⁡(z)r(z) can be represented as the logarithmic derivative of the polynomial
∏0≤k<N(z−ak)\prod_{0\leq k<N}(z-a_{k}); alternatively, the function r⁡(z)r(z) can be thought of as
the Cauchy transform of the sum of the Dirac measures of mass one at the points
a0,…,aN−1a_{0},\ldots,a_{N-1}. Another interpretation of the simplest fraction rr is that the value
r⁡(z)r(z) represents the complex conjugation of the electrostatic field at the point zz, caused by
charges placed at the points a0,…,aN−1a_{0},\ldots,a_{N-1}, assuming forces inversely proportional
to the distance.

The simplest fractions are an interesting and important object in various topics of contemporary
analysis. One can mention here, for example, that in 2006 J. M. Anderson and V. Eiderman []
solved a long-standing problem of Macintyre–Fuchs describing the growth of the Hausdorff content of
the level sets of simplest fractions. For a recent survey of numerous results on simplest fractions
see [].

G. R. Mac Lane [] initiated in 1949 the study of approximation by polynomials with
restriction on the location of their zeros. One says that a set EE is a polynomial approximation set
relative to a domain GG if every zero-free holomorphic function ff on GG can be approximated
uniformly on compact subsets of GG by polynomials having zeros only on EE. Mac Lane showed that for
every bounded (simply connected) Jordan domain Ω⊂ℂ\Omega\subset\mathbb{C} with rectifiable boundary,
∂Ω\partial\Omega is a polynomial approximation set relative to Ω\Omega. Later, M. Thompson
[], Chui [], and Z. Rubinstein and E. B. Saff [] strengthened this result by
considering bounded polynomial approximation in the unit disc.

Furthermore, J. Korevaar [] considered Mac Lane’s problem in a more general setting and
related it to approximation by simplest fractions, which is one of the main objects of consideration
in the present paper. The main result of Korevaar reads as follows:

###### Korevaar’s theorem.

Let GG be a bounded simply connected domain in ℂ\mathbb{C}, and let E⊂ℂE\subset\mathbb{C} be such
that E∩G=∅E\cap G=\emptyset. The following four statements are equivalent:

1) The set EE is a polynomial approximation set relative to GG.

2) For every point w∈Ew\in E, the function z↦(z−w)−1z\mapsto(z-w)^{-1} can be approximated locally
uniformly in GG by polynomials having zeros only on EE.

3) There exists a system of finite families {aN,k:0≤k<N}\{a_{N,k}\colon 0\leq k<N\} of points in EE,
such that

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
∑0≤k<N1z−aN,k→0\sum_{0\leq k<N}\frac{1}{z-a_{N,k}}\to 0
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────

locally uniformly in GG as N→∞N\to\infty.

4) The set closE\mathop{\rm clos}E separates the plane, and GG belongs to a bounded connected
component of the set ℂ∖closE\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathop{\rm clos}E.

Thus, the possibility of approximation in the sense of the first assertion of the theorem is
equivalent to the possibility of approximation of the zero function by simplest fractions with poles
on EE.

For a given set E⊂ℂE\subset\mathbb{C}, we consider the family of all simplest fractions with poles
on the set EE:

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
𝒮ℱ(E)={∑0≤k<N1z−aN,k:N≥1,aN,k∈E, 0≤k<N}.\SF(E)=\bigg\{\sum_{0\leq k<N}\frac{1}{z-a_{N,k}}:N\geq   
1,\,a_{N,k}\in E,\,0\leq k<N\bigg\}.                                                              
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

As a corollary of Korevaar’s theorem, one has the following result about approximation of general
holomorphic functions (not necessarily zero–free ones) by simplest fractions with restrictions on
the poles. Let GG be a bounded simply connected domain in ℂ\mathbb{C}, and let KK be a compact
subset of GG having connected complement. Then the family 𝒮ℱ⁡(∂G)\SF(\partial G) is dense in the
space A⁡(K)A(K) consisting of all continuous functions on KK which are holomorphic in the interior of
KK. Notice that Korevaar’s results were recently extended by P. A. Borodin [].

In the beginning of 1970-s, Chui [] considered yet another problem related with the
approximation by the simplest fractions with poles lying on the unit circle. He was interested in
the question whether the set 𝒮ℱ=𝒮ℱ⁡(𝕋)\SF=\SF(\mathbb{T}) is dense in the Bergman space A1=A1
(𝔻)A^{1}=A^{1}(\mathbb{D}) consisting of all functions holomorphic and integrable in 𝔻\mathbb{D},
and endowed with the usual L1L^{1}-norm (with respect to normalized planar Lebesgue measure m2m_{2}
on the unit disk, dm2(z)=π−1dxdydm_{2}(z)=\pi^{-1}\,dxdy, z=x+iyz=x+iy).

In connection with this question Chui formulated the following conjecture.

###### Chui’s Conjecture.

For any positive integer NN, and for any family of points {ak}0≤k<N\{a_{k}\}_{0\leq k<N} on the unit
circle, we have

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
‖∑0≤k<N1z−ak‖L1(𝔻)≥‖∑0≤k<N1z−e2πik/N‖L1(𝔻).\bigg\|\sum_{0\leq                                     
k<N}\frac{1}{z-a_{k}}\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{D})}\geq\bigg\|\sum_{0\leq k<N}\frac{1}{z-e^{2\pi     
ik/N}}\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{D})}.                                                                
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

For N≥1N\geq 1 we denote

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
ΨN(z)=∑0≤k<N1z−e2πik/N.\Psi_{N}(z)=\sum_{0\leq k<N}\frac{1}{z-e^{2\pi ik/N}}.
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

It can be easily verified (see []) that

─────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────
(1.1)│‖ΨN‖L1(𝔻)≥C\|\Psi_{N}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{D})}\geq C
─────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────

for some absolute constant C>0C>0. Thus, Chui’s conjecture would imply that the set 𝒮ℱ\SF is not
dense in A1A^{1}.

The next year after the publication of Chui’s conjecture, D. J. Newman [] proved that the
set 𝒮ℱ\SF is not dense in A1A^{1}. More precisely, he established that

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
‖∑0≤k<N1z−ak‖L1(𝔻)≥π18\bigg\|\sum_{0\leq                                                          
k<N}\frac{1}{z-a_{k}}\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{D})}\geq\dfrac{\pi}{18}                               
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

for any collection {ak}0≤k<N\{a_{k}\}_{0\leq k<N} of points on the unit circle.

Next, Chui studied in [] approximation by simplest fractions in Jordan domains in the complex
plane in the Bers spaces, that is, in the weighted L1L^{1}-spaces with weights
λD2−q\lambda_{D}^{2-q}, 0<q<∞0<q<\infty, where λD\lambda_{D} is the Poincaré metric for the domain
DD under consideration. It follows from estimate ([1.1]) that the set 𝒮ℱ\SF is not dense in the
Bers spaces in 𝔻\mathbb{D} for every 1<q≤21<q\leq 2. It is proved in [] that for any Jordan
domain DD and for every q>2q>2, the simplest fractions with poles on the boundary of DD are dense in
the respective Bers space in DD. The results of [] were later extended in [].

Despite considerable progress in our knowledge of simplest fractions properties, including
approximation ones, the original question posed by Chui remains open. In this paper, we resolve a
version of Chui’s conjecture in the context of weighted Bergman spaces of square integrable
functions, that is in the Hilbert space setting.

Throughout the paper we use the following notation: for positive AA and BB, A≲BA\lesssim B means
that there is a positive numerical constant CC such that A≤CBA\leq CB, while A≳BA\gtrsim B means
that B≲AB\lesssim A, and A≍BA\asymp B means that both A≲BA\lesssim B and B≲AB\lesssim A.
